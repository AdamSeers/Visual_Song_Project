About

Visual Song turns audio into a video where every musical pitch becomes a colored shape. The mapping isn't arbitrary — each sound frequency is doubled into the visible light spectrum, so the color you see has a defensible mathematical relationship to the note you're hearing.

01 the color mapping

A pitch like A4 vibrates at 440 Hz, far below anything our eyes can see. Doubling that frequency 40 times lands it in the visible light spectrum (roughly 400–790 THz). The result is run through Dan Bruton's wavelength-to-RGB algorithm to get a screen color. Because each octave doubles the frequency, every A — A2, A3, A4, A5 — comes out the same color: a particular orange-red.

02 the other dimensions

Loudness becomes brightness. Spectral purity becomes saturation — a sine wave is vibrant, a voice with overtones is softer. Timbre shapes the silhouette: mellow tones round off into circles, brighter tones sharpen into squares. The grid is fixed at eight slots, with vibrant new notes able to displace duller existing ones.

03 caveats

Pitch detection works best on clear, sustained, harmonic sounds. Vocals, strings, winds, and piano usually look great. Heavy distortion, dense polyphony, and percussion-heavy tracks are harder — the analysis filters percussion out, but anything else still has to fit through eight visible slots.
